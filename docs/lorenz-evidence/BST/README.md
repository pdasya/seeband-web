# Pb-substituted Bi₀.₄₈Sb₁.₅₂Te₃ at 480 K

Table 1 supplies the composition-dependent DOS masses, deformation potentials and acceptor densities for six samples. The independent-pocket acoustic calculation uses a 0.145 eV gap, 6 valence and 2 conduction valleys, isotropic single-valley masses and fixed net acceptors. The core supplies per-band transport moments. Feed the calculated conductivity fractions and Seebeck contrast into the open-circuit mixing expression. The output columns in results.csv are SI except the two Lorenz columns, which give L in units of 10⁻⁸ W Ω/K². Run `python check480.py` to print the six numerical rows.

At x = 0 and 0.02, the published Fig. 4(a) bipolar thermal conductivities are approximately 0.47 and 0.33 W/(m K). This replay gives 0.374 and 0.255 W/(m K), respectively. It captures the endpoint suppression (32% vs about 30%) and underestimates the absolute values by about 20–23%. The source estimates the bipolar term by modelling transport measurements. Its published endpoints do not determine total L. Our six total L values are predictions conditional on these pocket and scattering assumptions.
