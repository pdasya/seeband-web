# PbTe Lorenz number: source and implementation audit

## Findings

1. Figures 5 and 6 imply a density-order inconsistency under their stated common equilibrium p-type band model. Figure 6's dotted curves are Eq. (1), a conductivity-weighted average of band Lorenz numbers. At each endpoint temperature, intersect each Fig. 6 dotted ordinate with the published dotted curve in Fig. 5(c). Each gives a single displayed valence-side energy. Reading Fig. 5(c)'s solid trace at the same energies then independently predicts all three Fig. 6 solid values at 300 K within 0.00041 in units of 10⁻⁸ W Ω K⁻². At both temperatures the larger nominal hole density maps to a higher electron chemical potential. In a common equilibrium DOS, occupied conduction states increase and holes in valence states decrease with chemical potential. The required order for net holes runs the other way. At 850 K the reconstructed energies fall on the Figure 5 markers in blue, black, red order for the Figure 6 labels red, black, blue. The authors' high-density off-frame text value about 2.9 is consistent with the red energy marker in Figure 5(b). Independent sensitivity review found all three valence intervals distinct at 300 K, only the two extreme intervals reliably separated at 850 K. A conduction-side solution from Figure 5(b) has net electrons for the scalar bands. Actual cause (legend, density bookkeeping or another unpublished convention) remains undetermined.
2. A second discrepancy can be seen in Figure 5 alone. For parabolic acoustic bands Eq. (10) and Eqs. (12–14) give a transport distribution linear in kinetic energy. At each original 850 K Fig. 5(c) energy marker the dotted Lorenz number exceeds the maximum of all three individual band values computed with the published edges. An average with positive conductance weights cannot do this. See edge-bound.csv. Swapping labels, fitting relative mobilities, or changing constant multiplicities at these same energies cannot solve it. For example, at the black marker, the highest valence edge would have to reach at least approximately −0.279 eV under the same assumptions and fixed C edge. The printed highest valence edge is −0.360 eV. This bound is a diagnostic, not a proposed fitted edge. Changing the energy reference consistently for both bands and chemical potential has no physical effect.
3. Independent integration over the total transport distribution and independent density quadrature reproduce the externally assembled SeeBand curves for three hole densities at all 111 temperatures each. Maximum difference 7.82 × 10⁻¹⁴ in plotted units with matched constants. Using SciPy's constants gives 6.69 × 10⁻⁶ or less. This tests numerical equivalence for the selected assumptions and one effective channel per printed mass. Figure 6 is a theoretical prediction, not an experimental measurement of L.

## Equations and conventions

Relative to C, the edges are 0, −(0.09+0.0004T) eV for L and −0.36 eV for Σ. At 300 K the scalar masses for L, Σ, C are (0.36, 2, 0.30) electron masses. Eq. (38) scales L and C by 1+0.5(T−300)/T. Acoustic deformation potentials are (19,9.5,19) eV. The DOS includes spin once. At each temperature solve p_L+p_Σ−n_C equal to nominal doping. For isotropic parabolic bands, M(E) is proportional to m times kinetic energy. For intravalley acoustic phonons, mean free path is proportional to 1/(Tm²D²). Thus the common-energy-axis spectral conductance of band i scales as positive kinetic energy divided by m_i D_i², up to a common factor. No fitted amplitudes enter this comparison. The paper's Eq. (39), taken literally in SI, gives ħρc_s²/(πD²) with units s/m³; more information is needed to interpret it as a relaxation time. Eq. (37) also puts band convergence at 675 K, whereas the prose gives about 450 K. Neither ambiguity identifies the inputs used to generate Figure 5.

Write z=(E−μ)/(kBT) on one electron-energy axis. For K_j=∫Ξ(E) z^j (−∂f/∂E) dE, the open-circuit L=(kB/e)²[K₂/K₀−(K₁/K₀)²]. The equivalent bandwise sum is Σw_i L_i + Σw_i(S_i−S)², with S=Σw_i S_i. With the two holes combined into a group h, the positive variance separates into w_L w_Σ(S_L−S_Σ)²/w_h and w_h w_C(S_h−S_C)². The band's own L_i is independent of the constant amplitude of its spectral conductance. Pei SI Eq. S2 retains only Σw_i L_i and uses Kane L and C, parabolic Σ; compare this convention with the dotted baseline, subject to the different dispersions. Thesberg treats all three bands as parabolic. His Figure 6 solid curve includes both mixing terms.

With a Python environment containing numpy, scipy, pandas, matplotlib and pypdfium2, run the following in this directory.
