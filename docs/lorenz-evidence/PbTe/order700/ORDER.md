Run the baseline instructions in ../REPLAY.md, then run python order.py with NumPy and pandas. budget-700K.csv compares visible PDF paths by linear interpolation between adjacent original vertices. The decomposition chart covers all three densities from 300 to 850 K. Predictions beyond the plotted reference frame carry no graphical verification. The pinned-core replay regenerated seeband.csv byte-for-byte. All Lorenz values use units of 10⁻⁸ W Ω K⁻².

Increasing the net hole density from 1.5 to 2.5 × 10²⁰ cm⁻³ raises the calculated intraband sum by 0.053, while the electron–hole mixing correction drops by 0.265. The hole–hole correction changes by less than 0.001. Together these produce a −0.213 change. On the published paths the dotted intraband sum falls by 0.049 and the combined mixing term increases by 0.189, yielding +0.140. The change in the mixing slope is the largest discrepancy and even the baseline has the opposite slope. Figure 6 does not resolve the separate electron–hole and hole–hole pieces. Electron–hole here means treating both hole bands as a group.

In the calculation the chemical potential moves deeper into the valence states, the electron conductivity fraction falls from 0.514% to 0.170%, and the electron–hole Seebeck separation grows only slightly. This is sufficient to explain the sign of the calculated slope. The saved fixed-energy slices in ../formula-audit/slices.csv illustrate an ambiguity on the reference side. At 850 K the blue 2.5 marker on Figure 5(c) yields about 2.21; the red 1.5 marker on Figure 5(b) yields about 2.90. The prose accompanying Figure 6 attributes about 2.9 to the blue line. These are different comparisons unless chemical potentials are held at those markers. The paper does not publish the three numerical chemical-potential trajectories. This observation alone cannot establish a swapped legend. The printed gap law and the approximate crossing temperature in the prose also disagree. We used the printed law, without tuning.
