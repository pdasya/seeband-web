# Lorenz number in SeeBand · evidence for scientific review

**What can we reproduce?** The SI Lorenz number of the original bipolar demo follows independently from carrier balance and transport moments. The two Hattori benchmarks support its predicted mixing response on the parts of the figures that can be read. Two comparisons remain open: the ordering of the PbTe curves in Thesberg Fig. 6 and the high-chemical-potential tail of the Hattori S4 Seebeck curve. Each study below includes its assumptions, original source, numerical evidence and a way to inspect the result without running the website.

## From transport moments to the measured quantity

Use one electron-energy axis, with z = (E − μ)/(kBT) and Kⱼ = ∫ Ξ(E) zʲ (−∂f/∂E) dE for the total spectral conductance. The open-circuit Lorenz number is L = (kB/e)² [K₂/K₀ − (K₁/K₀)²], in W Ω/K². With wᵢ = σᵢ/Σσᵢ and S = Σwᵢ Sᵢ this gives

**L = Σwᵢ Lᵢ + Σwᵢ(Sᵢ − S)².**

For two bands the second term is w₁w₂(S₁ − S₂)², with Sᵢ in V/K. It describes heat carried by opposing partial electric currents whose sum is zero. It survives for two hole bands with unequal thermopowers. With one electron and one hole band it is the bipolar term. The electronic thermal conductivity follows as κₑ = LσT. Reconstructing L from an independently plotted κₑ and σ tests a published curve. Forming that ratio from the site's own calculated outputs checks internal consistency.

In an ordinary degenerate metal each broad band has weak electron–hole asymmetry across the thermal window. Its thermopower is small, so the variance can remain small even when many bands cross the Fermi level. In the synthetic example the opposing band thermopowers differ by 558 μV/K and both bands conduct appreciably. The extra heat term then exceeds the single-band Sommerfeld scale. These statements concern independent-band electronic transport under the stated scattering assumptions. [PB moments](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-03760-4) · [Multiband theory and PbTe](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.95.125206)

## Why SeeBand gives 739 μV/K

At T = 900 K, ΔE = +0.10 eV and μ = +0.60 eV both valence maxima are below μ. SeeBand integrates the complete acoustic transport spectrum and gives 738.94 μV/K. Direct numerical quadrature of the same specified spectrum independently reproduces the core. The S4 line reads 697.6 [685.3, 708.3] μV/K. With a symmetric ±10 kBT integration window the diagnostic gives 698.07 μV/K because it removes part of the transported-energy tail. The paper writes infinite integration limits. The authors’ actual numerical range and original tabulated curves would distinguish this explanation from a different implementation convention. The primary prediction and residuals retain the full integral. [Hattori S4](Hattori-S4/REVIEW.md)

## How to review

Start with a study report and its CSVs. Reported graphical envelopes describe source resolution and overlap. A shared normalization point, overlapping strokes and rows interpolated between source points do not count as independent evidence. To inspect the PbTe inversion or the largest S4 residual, use the per-point data and the corresponding source figure linked by that study. Original publishers host the papers.

Most recent core comparisons used [84af5de](https://github.com/pdasya/seeband-web/commit/84af5de52675c45ebbc139ec517873c573a4effa). That core derives L as κₑ/(σT) and predates the moment-based L of #293. On the branch of #293, merged with main, `synthetic/peak.py` reproduces [its recorded results](synthetic/peak-results.txt) exactly, with relative errors of 6.2 × 10⁻¹⁴ or less; the other studies were not rerun on that core. The Mg₂Sn replay records its earlier revision, and the Kane design reference pins its own scripts. Three-band PbTe calculations assemble individual band outputs externally. Running the production website and fitting material parameters are separate from these replay experiments. Absolute model output, a ratio used by an author and L/L₀ have different units or denominators; each study records the quantity it compares. [Absolute-L implementation history and evidence](https://github.com/pdasya/seeband-web/issues/292#issuecomment-7202165257)

## Checks repeated for this PR

A clean Python 3.13.18 environment with the pinned repository core reran the Hattori S4 and S7 forward calculations and independent moment checks, the S4 residual table, and the PbTe core and direct moment calculations. Disposable copies kept the published digitizations separate from regenerated results. With the pinned core the local API matched the 50-point export to 2.43 × 10⁻¹² relative or better in all three Lorenz columns. The maximum relative residual in κₑ = LσT was 2.23 × 10⁻¹⁶. The synthetic, Kane, PbTe and figure-specific reports distinguish historical checks and their revisions from these reruns. No source curve was refitted.
