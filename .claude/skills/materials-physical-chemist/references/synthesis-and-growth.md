# Synthesis of polycrystals and growth of single crystals

## Chemical and thermodynamic starting point

Identify target phase/composition, allowed substitutions and valences, relevant phase diagram, precursor form/purity, atmosphere, accessible equipment, sample quantity and quality target. Distinguish chemical homogeneity, phase purity, density, orientation, domain structure and defect concentration; each has its own acceptance measurement.

Balance precursor reactions, including carbonate/water/oxygen losses, redox and expected by-products. Convert amounts with declared purity, hydration and composition assumptions. For nonstoichiometric oxides identify charge compensation and oxygen exchange rather than silently rounding the formula to ideal stoichiometry.

## Polycrystalline synthesis

| Route                                          | Selection question and practical checks                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ceramic solid-state reaction                   | Are precursor stability, contact area and diffusion adequate? Track mixing, milling contamination, particle size, pellet dimensions, intermediate phases and any regrind/anneal cycles.                                |
| Arc/induction melting of metals/intermetallics | Are the components compatible with melting, atmosphere and volatility? Track mass before/after, melt homogeneity, segregation, reactive losses and subsequent annealing. Repeated melting is not proof of homogeneity. |
| Sealed-tube/chalcogenide/pnictide synthesis    | What vapor species/pressures, gas evolution and container reactions occur throughout the program? Use system-specific evidence for charge, headspace, staged reaction and containment.                                 |
| Solution-derived oxide precursors              | Can precursor mixing reduce reaction distances without leaving unwanted anions, carbon or secondary phases? Track precursor chemistry and decomposition before final crystallization.                                  |
| Pressure-assisted sintering/hot pressing/SPS   | Is the aim densification, phase reaction or grain control? Track actual sample temperature, tooling contamination, pressure, atmosphere and gradients; density alone does not prove phase purity.                      |
| Mechanochemical/metathesis routes              | Which intermediate or metastable state is intended? Track milling dose/media, heat release, contamination, washing and post-treatment.                                                                                 |

Treat calcination, reaction annealing, densification and grain growth as distinct steps. Choose holds from known reaction/phase/volatility constraints and kinetic length scales; an arbitrary longer/hotter hold can worsen phase stability or composition. Use intermediate PXRD, mass changes and representative SEM/composition maps to diagnose incomplete reaction versus segregation or container contamination.

## Choosing a crystal-growth route

- **Congruent melting and manageable volatility:** assess Bridgman/VGF, Czochralski or floating-zone methods against melt stability, temperature, container compatibility and available thermal fields.
- **Incongruent/peritectic melting:** investigate primary crystallization fields and suitable solution compositions. Flux/self-flux, traveling-solvent or other solution-zone methods may access the target; stoichiometric melt freezing is not a default solution.
- **Flux growth:** choose solvent from solubility, competing phases, incorporation/contamination, volatility, wetting and separation chemistry. Plan nucleation/seed strategy, supersaturation and growth window; verify crystals are the target rather than crystallized flux or another primary phase.
- **CVT/vapor growth:** require plausible reversible transport reactions and gas-species equilibria for the chosen transport agent. Establish source and growth temperatures from the chemistry; do not assume a universal hot-to-cold direction. Check transport-agent incorporation and parasitic wall nucleation.
- **Hydrothermal/solution growth:** assess solvent/mineralizer chemistry, solubility gradient, redox, vessel/liner compatibility and actual pressure/temperature envelope. Supply exact operating conditions only from applicable evidence and equipment constraints.

## Interface stability and chemical uniformity

Declare temperature-gradient and growth-rate definitions, measurement locations and units. Interface stability depends on the thermal field, solutal boundary layer, liquidus slope, segregation, convection and geometry. A G/v ratio alone is not a universal stability criterion; use a quantitative criterion only with its assumptions and material parameters.

Keep equilibrium partition coefficient k0 distinct from effective incorporation keff. Scheil/BPS-type segregation models require explicit mixing and solid-diffusion assumptions. Map seed/middle/tail and centre/edge rather than reporting one composition as representative of a boule.

Separate excessive nucleation, constitutional instability, inclusions, crucible reaction, thermal stress/cracking and post-growth transformations. Correlate growth logs with spatial defects; change a variable that distinguishes competing causes, then verify the predicted consequence.

## Containers, atmosphere and temperature schedules

Assess reaction with silica, alumina, BN, graphite, refractory metals or noble-metal crucibles for the actual melt/flux and oxygen activity. There is no universally inert container. Purified argon is not by itself a defined reducing potential. State gas composition/flow or sealed headspace and distinguish furnace setpoint from calibrated sample temperature.

For unknown compositions or absent phase/compatibility information, give a conditional route-selection and small-scale characterization plan. Exact temperatures, gradients, pull rates, pressure and sealed-vessel loading need material-specific literature and apparatus evidence; label proposed screening values as proposals. Do not invent an executable recipe to fill those gaps. Address concrete pressure, volatile/toxic-species or reactive-mixture constraints when that chemistry is present.

## Reproducible result

Record precursor lots/purity, weighed and recovered masses, charge/container geometry, atmosphere, full ramp/hold/cooling/quench program, mixing/seed/orientation details, washing/flux removal and storage. Verify phase constitution, actual composition and spatial quality after the relevant final treatment. Independent synthesis runs measure reproducibility; repeated scans of one specimen measure repeatability.
