# Select a method that tests the claim

Start from the unresolved claim, its competing explanations, the required length/time scale and the expected discriminating signal. Choose the smallest useful set of measurements with adequate sensitivity. Add an orthogonal method when it resolves a real ambiguity; a fixed quota of techniques does not establish validity.

| Claim                              | Useful evidence and controls                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Phase identity/fractions           | Whole-pattern PXRD with candidate models and calibration; representative microscopy/chemistry; standards if amorphous or absolute fractions matter.                                          |
| Average structure/site order       | SC-XRD or suitable powder refinement; neutron/resonant contrast when atom contrast is insufficient; occupancies constrained by independent composition where justified.                      |
| Local distortion/disorder          | Total-scattering PDF, EXAFS, diffuse scattering or local electron diffraction with model/range checks; compare to the average structure.                                                     |
| Crystal orientation/domain quality | Laue plus spatial sampling; rocking curves/reciprocal-space maps, EBSD, topography or TEM chosen for the suspected domain/defect scale.                                                      |
| Bulk stoichiometry                 | Matrix-appropriate calibrated bulk chemistry (e.g. ICP-OES/MS after validated dissolution); recovery, blanks and contamination controls. Spatially resolved EPMA/EDS tests homogeneity.      |
| Oxygen/nonstoichiometry or valence | Calibrated redox titration/TGA under defined gas chemistry, suitable neutron contrast or XANES/EELS/XPS with their depth/model limitations. Charge balance alone is a hypothesis.            |
| Thermal event/phase boundary       | DSC/DTA/TGA with atmosphere, rate, calibration, blank and heating/cooling/repeat history; in situ diffraction or quenched products to assign the event.                                      |
| Surface chemistry                  | XPS/AES/SIMS or appropriate spectroscopy with depth sensitivity, referencing, sputtering effects and exposure history. Surface composition is distinct from bulk composition.                |
| Microstructure/density/porosity    | SEM/EBSD/TEM with sampling and feature definitions; geometric/Archimedes/pycnometry methods according to open/closed porosity and wetting constraints.                                       |
| Functional property mechanism      | Measure transport, magnetism, heat capacity, optical or mechanical response on characterized, comparable material; account for geometry, contacts, density, texture, impurities and history. |

## Thermal analysis

Distinguish onset, peak maximum and integrated heat; state heating/cooling rate, gas composition/flow, sample mass, pan/container, calibration, baseline and integration limits. TGA measures mass change rather than the identity of an evolved species. Couple gas analysis or phase/chemistry checks when distinguishing volatilization, oxidation, dehydration and decomposition.

A DSC feature may be melting, reaction, structural/magnetic/electronic change or an artifact. Hysteresis and scan-rate dependence help diagnose kinetics but do not alone prove a specific reaction or transition order. Cooling exotherms can be ordinary crystallization/solidification; determine the process from supporting evidence. Reheating can change or destroy a metastable initial state, so identify the state each scan represents.

## Spectroscopy and physical properties

For Raman/IR use selection rules, polarization, calibration, laser heating/fluorescence and possible secondary phases; a peak assignment is not automatically a unique structure determination. For XPS specify energy referencing/charging and fitting constraints; neither a fixed adventitious-carbon energy nor an unverified lattice peak supplies a universal absolute reference. Sputtering can change composition/valence.

For XAS distinguish local coordination and oxidation-state proxies from a unique bulk phase assignment. For transport distinguish intrinsic bulk behavior from grain boundaries, porosity and contact/geometry effects. For impedance spectroscopy, equivalent-circuit fits require identifiable components and a physical electrode/grain/interfacial interpretation. Magnetometry and heat capacity can detect transitions or minor functional phases without by themselves locating their chemical origin.

## Reconcile conflicting probes

Compare the same batch, treatment, temperature, spatial region, sampling depth and statistical unit. Then test resolution, contrast, preferred orientation, normalization and model assumptions. A local measurement and a bulk average can both be correct. State which conclusion remains supported, which is conditional, and which next measurement most efficiently separates the alternatives.
