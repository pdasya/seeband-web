# User guide

This guide walks through SeeBand Web on the example of the full-Heusler
compound Fe₂VAl₀.₉Si₀.₁: loading data, fitting one band to the Seebeck
coefficient, fitting two bands to S, ρ and $R_H$, and reading and exporting
the results. It is adapted from the
[user guide of the desktop SeeBand](https://github.com/xAngelswordx/SeeBand/blob/803b0e7a425a0a3f4f59688df77e3ad283ca49a1/UserGuide_SeeBand.pdf)
by Michael Parzer and Alexander Riss, published under the MIT licence. The
method is described in the SeeBand paper [[1]](#references), and the formulas
behind each step are on the [Conventions](/conventions/) page. The desktop
SeeBand's [licence](https://github.com/xAngelswordx/SeeBand/blob/803b0e7a425a0a3f4f59688df77e3ad283ca49a1/license.txt)
covers its documentation as well as its code.

The app writes numbers with a decimal comma, as in 0,06 or 2,68; this guide
writes them with a decimal point.

The example files are `01_Fe2VAl09Si01_see.txt`, `_rho.txt` and `_hall.txt`
in the [examples of the desktop SeeBand](https://github.com/xAngelswordx/SeeBand/tree/803b0e7a425a0a3f4f59688df77e3ad283ca49a1/data/Examples).
The screenshots and numbers below come from runs of this version on them; a
later version can give other numbers, and
[Results that changed](/results-changed/) says why.

## Load data

The fit screen opens with the Data panel. Load each quantity from its own file
under **Separate files**, or one table whose columns you assign under
**Single file**. A file has the temperature in one column and the quantity in
another; columns are separated by spaces, tabs or commas.

The example files have no column names, so for each the app asks what its
columns hold: choose the quantity of the second column, and the file loads.

Units do not have to be SI. Each column's unit is read from its header, such
as `S [uV/K]`, or chosen from the list beside it; with neither, the value is
read in SI and the unit is marked as assumed, with a dashed outline around it.
The desktop examples have no header and are in SI, so their units are assumed
and correct. **Load σ instead** reads conductivity in place of resistivity,
used as ρ = 1/σ. The Hall file of the example repeats 100 K, which its card
notes.

The **Interpolation** switch replaces each quantity's points, for the fit, by
a degree-10 Chebyshev polynomial at about one point per 20 K. This keeps a
temperature interval with many points from weighing more than the rest. It
stays within each quantity's measured range and never extrapolates; the
interpolated points are not independent measurements.

## One band: the Seebeck coefficient

Choose **SPB** and the fit mode **S**. For a single parabolic band, S depends
only on the Fermi energy $E_F$ and on the sign of the band mass m, which makes
the band a conduction (m > 0) or a valence band (m < 0). The size of m does
not change S, so it is not fitted in this mode.

One band does not describe the example over the whole range: more bands take
part at higher temperatures, as the Fermi–Dirac distribution broadens. Under
**Fit range, K**, set 5 and 240 K, so that only the points below 240 K enter
the fit; the grey shading on the chart shows what is left out. Then press
**Run fit**.

With the default positive mass, a conduction band, which matches the negative
S of the data, the fit gives $E_F$ = 641 K, which is 0.055 eV, inside the
conduction band. The sign of the mass is your choice, not the fit's: with a
negative mass and a positive $E_F$ the band holds no carriers at T = 0, and
the calculation says so.
The field shows 0,06 eV, at its step of 0.01 eV; **Parameters to file** gives
the full value. The desktop guide gave 0.059 eV for the same 641 K, which was
a conversion error: 641 K × $k_B$ = 0.0552 eV.

**Edit bounds**, the gear beside Parameters, sets the range each parameter may
take in a fit. To keep a parameter at its value, lock it with the padlock
beside it instead of narrowing its bounds.

## Two bands: Seebeck, resistivity and Hall

Choose **DPB** and the fit mode **S + ρ + $R_H$**. The two-band model has a
valence band 1, with its top at zero, and a band 2 at $E_g$. Its parameters
are:

- $N_1$ and $N_2$, the band degeneracies, which are not fitted;
- $m_2/m_1$, the band mass ratio, whose sign sets the type of band 2;
- $E_g$, the position of band 2, and $E_F$, the Fermi energy at T = 0;
- for acoustic phonons or ionized impurities, A, the conductivity scale, and
  B, band 2's scale relative to band 1's; for alloy disorder, F and G instead;
- $m_1$, the density-of-states mass of band 1, which only $R_H$ sets; it is the single-valley mass when $N_1 = 1$.

Choose the scattering mechanism under **Scattering**: acoustic phonons, alloy
disorder or ionized impurities. One mechanism applies over the whole range.
The desktop's combination of acoustic phonons and alloy disorder by
Matthiessen's rule is not offered.

For acoustic phonons and alloy disorder, loading S fills eligible starting
values from a neural net and preserves values you entered by hand. Choose
**Initial guess** to replace a free manual start with the estimate. Held and
fixed parameters, including $N_1$ and $N_2$, and fit bounds keep their values. Until you
fit, the badge beside **Fit** names the net's temperature range; afterwards
the fit log's first step does, here 400–700 K. The fit of S is the most
delicate step, as its parameters set the electronic structure, so a good start
matters. The fit range stays as you set it for one band, so set it back to 50
and 800 K, the range of the run below, and leave **Interpolation** off. The
**Fitting method** is Verified sequential, the default. Press **Run fit**.

On the example, with acoustic phonons on 50–800 K, the fit follows S closely,
with an RMSE of 2.68 µV/K. It ends at its limit of 20 iterations, so these values are not a
converged result, as the badge and the notes say. The same fit of S and ρ
alone, whose limit is 1000 iterations, settles at the same S and ρ.

The resistivity deviates at low temperatures (RMSE 9.8 %): with acoustic
phonons alone, ρ goes to 0 as T → 0, while the data keep a residual ρ of about
5 × 10⁻⁷ Ω·m. The fit of $R_H$ changes only $m_1$, which sets the size of
$R_H$; here it ends at 9.0 $m_e$, near its upper bound of 10, so the data do
not set it as a mass. The model has the right sign and size of $R_H$ below
about 300 K but falls too slowly above it, and the RMSE of 104 % comes mostly
from the high-temperature points, where $R_H$ is small; its relative L2,
under **Numerical diagnostics**, is 22 %. A fit of $R_H$ tests the temperature
dependence that S and ρ set, and this one does not confirm it. B ends at
1.096, against its upper bound of 1.1.

The band diagram is a sketch, as on the desktop: its caution says that its
curves do not follow E = ħ²k²/(2m) in stated units, which
[#108](https://github.com/pdasya/seeband-web/issues/108) is to fix. Only $E_F$
and the band edges are exact.

## Results

**Open results**, or **Results** in the header, gives the fit on one page:
the model, scattering and RMSE of each quantity, the parameters with what each
means and how it was found, and the fit log, which lists the solver runs in
their order and how the fit stopped.

A parameter's status says what the fit can tell about it: estimated, at
bound, not determined by the data, did not move from its start or not
assessed; held, for a parameter you locked; fixed, as the degeneracies are;
or not fitted yet, or not fitted because the fit failed. None of these says
that a value is right.
Uncertainties are not assessed. A fit that converges does not show that the
model suits the sample: check the RMSE, the curves against the data, and any
parameter at its bound.

**Earlier runs**, on the results page, keeps up to 10 fits of the session in
the browser, and compares two of them side by side. Opening a session file
starts with none.

### Additional graphs

The additional graphs, on the results page and under **More graphs** on the
fit screen, are calculated from the values in use, for what the fit mode
allows: μ(T) in every mode, σ, $\kappa_e$ and the Lorenz number in modes with
ρ, and the band contributions and carrier concentrations for two bands.

<figure>
  <a href="/guide/additional-graphs.png">
    <img
      src="/guide/additional-graphs.png"

      alt="Six additional graphs: chemical potential, conductivity with 1/ρ of the data, electronic thermal conductivity, Lorenz number, band contributions to S and carrier concentration by band."
    />

  <figcaption>
    Additional graphs from the built-in synthetic DPB demo. The Lorenz panel
    shows total L and both band values.
  </figcaption>
</figure>

- **Chemical potential μ(T).** μ falls with temperature, from 0.11 eV to just
  above the edge of the conduction band at 800 K. The gap, 0.055 eV, is smaller
  than $k_BT$ at the upper temperatures, so holes form in the valence band. This
  changes every transport coefficient.
- **Band contributions.** Each band's S is weighted by its conductivity, so
  the two contributions add up to S:

  $$
  S_{\mathrm{cont},i} = \frac{S_i\,\sigma_i}{\sigma_1 + \sigma_2}.
  $$

- **Electronic thermal conductivity $\kappa_e$.** It is often estimated by the
  Wiedemann–Franz law, $\kappa_e = L\sigma T$, with the Lorenz number of
  degenerate metals, $L_0$ = 2.44 × 10⁻⁸ W·Ω/K², or with an estimate from the
  Seebeck coefficient such as that of Kim et al. [[2]](#references). Here L follows from the band
  parameters, and the model computes $\kappa_e=L\sigma T$. For two bands $\kappa_e$ includes the bipolar part. It arises
  when two bands with different S conduct in parallel; with a conduction and a
  valence band it is carried by electron–hole pairs that form at the hot side
  and recombine at the cold side, releasing the gap energy. It can raise
  $\kappa_e$ markedly at high temperatures. In the Fe₂VAl example, total L rises from
  about 2.1 × 10⁻⁸ W·Ω/K² at 200 K to about 6.6 × 10⁻⁸ W·Ω/K² at 800 K. The bipolar part
  cannot be measured on its own; a two-band fit gives a model-dependent
  estimate of it, only as good as the fit. The lattice part is not modelled.
- **Lorenz number L(T)** shows total L and each band’s own L in units of
  10⁻⁸ W·Ω/K². The download gives absolute values in W·Ω/K².
- **Carrier concentration by band.** Without $R_H$ data the size of $m_1$ is
  not measured, so these values are relative; compare the bands, not the values.
  Here $m_1$ is near its bound, so treat them as relative too. Band 1's holes
  are drawn as negative, and the ratio of the bands depends on $N_2/N_1$.

The formulas are in [Conventions](/conventions/#thermal-conductivity-and-lorenz-number).

## Export and sessions

- **Data to file** saves the data, the curve of the start values and the fit
  curve, as text, a CSV table or a ZIP of plain tables with the additional
  graphs and the session.
- **Parameters to file** saves a line for each parameter, with its unit and
  status, as text or a CSV table.
- **Session to file** saves the data, with the full text of each file, the
  settings and the last fit, as one file; **Open session**, under the data,
  loads it back.
- **Report (PDF)**, on the results page, prints the results.

## What differs from the desktop guide

- Data can be loaded in other units, as one table or as σ instead of ρ.
- Scattering offers ionized impurities, and no longer the combination of
  acoustic phonons and alloy disorder.
- The default fitting method, Verified sequential, checks each step
  numerically; **Legacy sequential (reproduce earlier results)**, under
  **Fitting method**, reproduces the desktop's fitting.
- A parameter is held with its padlock rather than by narrow bounds.
- Some known errors of the desktop are corrected, so results can differ from it
  on purpose: see [Results that changed](/results-changed/).
- 641 K is 0.055 eV, not 0.059 eV, and the electronic thermal conductivity is
  $\kappa_e$ throughout, not also λ.
- The desktop guide's unresolved references are given in full where they could
  be identified. Two could not be, and are left out: a chapter of an unnamed
  reference, for examples of how the parameters change the transport
  properties, and the source it gave for the electron–hole coupling behind the
  bipolar part.
