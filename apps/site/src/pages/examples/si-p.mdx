# Si:P: a gap outside the default bounds

## Data

Phosphorus-doped silicon, from the files
[`02_SiP_see.txt`, `_rho.txt` and `_hall.txt`](https://github.com/xAngelswordx/SeeBand/tree/803b0e7a425a0a3f4f59688df77e3ad283ca49a1/data/Examples)
of the desktop SeeBand: 12 points of S from 308 to 650 K, 19 of ρ from 338 to
654 K and 10 of $R_H$ from 304 to 654 K, all inside the default fit range of
50–800 K. The SeeBand paper [[1]](#references) analyses Si:P with the same
two-band model and cites its measurements to Agrawal, de Boor and Dasgupta
[[2]](#references).

No neural net covers 308–650 K, so **Initial guess** offers no start. Settings
not named here are the [defaults](/examples/#reading-these-pages).

## With the default bounds

DPB, fit mode S + ρ, acoustic phonons, and the default start: $m_2/m_1$ =
1.01, $E_g$ = 0.09 eV, $E_F$ = −0.01 eV, A = 40 000, B = 1. The bounds of
$E_g$ and $E_F$ are −0.26 to 0.26 eV (±3000 K), about a quarter of the gap of
silicon.

| Status                | RMSE S   | RMSE ρ | $E_g$             | $E_F$                                 | Also at a bound |
| --------------------- | -------- | ------ | ----------------- | ------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Numerical fit stalled | 545 µV/K | 52 %   | 0.26 eV, at bound | 0.01 eV, “not determined by the data” | B = 1.1         |

This fit is a failure, and the screen says so: the status, $E_g$ and B at

place it. The fit with wider bounds below suggests that the gap the model needs
lies outside the default bounds.

## With wider bounds

**Edit bounds** sets $E_g$ to 0.5–1.5 eV and $E_F$ to from −1.5 to 1.6 eV. The
start is then typed into the parameter fields: $m_2/m_1$ = 0.74, $E_g$ = 1.08
eV and $E_F$ = 1.0805 eV, 6 K above the conduction band edge; the field shows
it as 1,08. A, B and $m_1$ keep their defaults of 40 000, 1 and 1 mₑ. The gap
of 1.08 eV is the value the SeeBand paper gives; $m_2/m_1$ = 0.74 was chosen by
hand.

| Fit mode      | Status              | RMSE S    | RMSE ρ | RMSE $R_H$ | $m_2/m_1$ | $E_g$   | $m_1$      | At a bound |
| ------------- | ------------------- | --------- | ------ | ---------- | --------- | ------- | ---------- | ---------- |
| S + ρ         | numerically settled | 54.7 µV/K | 23.8 % | not fitted | 0.758     | 1.04 eV | not fitted | B = 1.1    |
| S + ρ + $R_H$ | numerically settled | 54.7 µV/K | 23.8 % | 34.0 %     | 0.758     | 1.04 eV | 1.33 mₑ    | B = 1.1    |

**Parameters to file** gives the values in full: $E_g$ = 1.044351 eV and
$E_F$ = 1.044377 eV, so $E_F$ ends 0.30 K (26 µeV) above the conduction band
edge, having started 6 K above it. Both fits took 2 iterations.

### Other starts

The start matters near the band edge. In the app, with the same bounds and
S + ρ + $R_H$:

| $E_F$ start          | Method              | Outcome                                                                                          |
| -------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1.08 eV, at the edge | Verified sequential | Failed                                                                                           |
| 1.09 eV, 116 K above | Verified sequential | Failed                                                                                           |
| 1.08 eV, at the edge | Legacy sequential   | stopped after 2 iterations with A, B and $m_1$ at their bounds, $E_g$ = 1.35 eV, RMSE S 162 µV/K |

A check with `seeband_core` directly, S + ρ with the same bounds and $E_F$
started 5.8 K above the band edge, ended at the same values from $E_g$ = 1.0,
1.08 and 1.2 eV with $m_2/m_1$ = 0.5, 0.74, 1.01 and 1.5, except from
$E_g$ = 1.0 eV with $m_2/m_1$ = 0.5, which failed. Started 0.03, 0.3, 1 or 3 K
above the edge, $E_F$ also returned to 0.30 K above it. So the result does not
depend on the paper's gap as a start, but it does need $E_F$ started just above
the edge.

## Interpretation

**The gap.** With room for it, the fit puts the gap at 1.04 eV, with B at its
upper bound. A check with `seeband_core` with B allowed up to 10 gives
0.99 eV, B at 10 and nearly the same RMSE of S, so the gap is not determined to
better than about 0.05 eV: it trades off with B. The model's gap does not
change with temperature, while silicon's falls from 1.12 eV at 300 K to about
1.02–1.05 eV at 550–650 K (Varshni's relation), where intrinsic conduction sets
in. 1.04 eV is in that range: an effective gap near the onset. The paper's
1.08 eV comes from the same model on the same data, so it is not an
independent check.

**The Fermi energy.** The model has no donor level, so an $E_F$ just above the
conduction band edge only sets the net donor density. 0.30 K above the edge,
with $m_2 = 0.758 × 1.33$ mₑ, is about 6 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻³; $1/(e|R_H|)$ at 304 K is
4.9 × 10¹⁴ cm⁻³. The starts above show the fit returns to this offset, so the
data determine it, given the other values as fitted.

**$R_H$.** In this method $R_H$ is fitted last and only scales $m_1$, so S and
ρ, and every other value, stay as the S + ρ fit left them; that is the
method's design, not a finding. The shape of $R_H$ is therefore a prediction
from S and ρ, and it misses by 34 %. $m_1$ = 1.33 mₑ makes the model's density
match $R_H$; with $N_1 = N_2 = 1$ it is not a band mass of silicon, whose
conduction band has six valleys.

**The fit.** The model describes the onset of intrinsic conduction: the rise
and fall of ρ and the steps in S and $R_H$. It is not a close fit: the RMSE of
S is 55 µV/K, most of it below 450 K, and of ρ 24 %.

## Limits

- B ends at its upper bound of 1.1, and the bound also moves $E_g$ and
  $m_2/m_1$ (see above). Whether B's default bounds of 0.9–1.1 suit silicon
  was not assessed.
- $N_1 = N_2 = 1$, so the masses are density-of-states masses for one valley.
- Possible reasons for the misfit, none tested here: no phonon drag, which is
  large in lightly doped silicon near 300 K; acoustic-phonon mobility goes as
  $T^{-3/2}$, while that of silicon's electrons falls faster; and a gap fixed
  in temperature.
- $|R_H|$ falls about elevenfold from 304 to 654 K, and its RMSE, in percent
  of each value, weighs the small high-temperature values as much as the
  large ones.
- Uncertainties are not assessed, and a physicist has not confirmed these
  results.

## Reproduce

Default bounds:

1. Open **Model fit**, choose **DPB**, then **S + ρ**.
2. Load `02_SiP_see.txt` and choose S for its second column; load
   `02_SiP_rho.txt` and choose ρ.
3. Press **Run fit**.

Wider bounds, once with **S + ρ** and the two files above, once with
**S + ρ + R_H** and also `02_SiP_hall.txt`, with $R_H$ for its second column:

1. Reload the page, choose **DPB** and the fit mode, and load the files.
2. Open **Edit bounds**, set $E_g$ to 0,5 and 1,5 and $E_F$ to −1,5 and 1,6,
   and press **Done**.
3. Type 0,74 for $m_2/m_1$, 1,08 for $E_g$ and 1,0805 for $E_F$, each followed
   by Enter.
4. Press **Run fit**, then **Parameters to file** for the full values.

For the other starts, type 1,08 or 1,09 for $E_F$ in step 3, and for the last
choose **Legacy sequential** under **Fitting method**.
