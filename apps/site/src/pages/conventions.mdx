# Conventions and model

Units, formulas and assumptions of the parabolic band models, as the calculation
core computes them. Formulas are checked against the core's code; where the
core differs from the desktop SeeBand, the core is what is described here, and
[Results that changed](/results-changed/) lists why. A physicist has not yet
confirmed the corrections.

## Explicit double parabolic bands

In core 0.4.0, choose VB+VB, VB+CB or CB+CB before fitting. The first edge
defines zero energy, and the offset is the second edge minus the first.
Changing the sign of the offset preserves the band labels. In VB+CB, a
positive offset opens a gap and a negative offset gives band overlap.

The band types and Fermi energy determine the carrier excess; no separate
p/n choice is needed. Earlier explicitly signed requests can still supply
**p** or **n**, and retain their carrier-sign checks. Enter the
zero-temperature Fermi energy relative to the first band edge, the
single-valley mass of band 1 in electron masses, the positive mass ratio
and both positive integer valley multiplicities (excluding spin). The model counts the
occupied states to determine the excess $n-p$ at that trial point. It then
solves for the chemical potential that preserves this excess at each
temperature. A mixed pair permits either sign. Within an intrinsic gap,
all Fermi energies between the band edges give the same transport curves.

The total Seebeck coefficient is conductivity-weighted and each weak-field
Hall contribution is weighted by the square of its conductivity fraction.
The electronic thermal conductivity includes the interband term whenever
the single-band Seebeck coefficients differ. The scattering choices
are acoustic phonons, alloy disorder, their combination and ionized impurities. Use manual
starting values and bounds for the explicit model.

For acoustic phonons and alloy disorder, the dimensionless Hall moment is
$\int_0^\infty \sqrt{x}(-\partial f/\partial x)\,dx = F_{-1/2}(\eta)/2$,
where $F$ is the unnormalized Fermi integral. P18 uses $x=t^2$ when the
integration window includes the band edge, so the derivative of the square
root at zero does not limit the accuracy of the quadrature.

With S or S+ρ the mass scale stays fixed. With S+ρ+Hall it can be fitted,
which sets the absolute concentration scale. At fixed Fermi energy and
mass ratio, rescaling both masses preserves the balance for the same
chemical potential and changes the Hall amplitude.
Opening an old S or S+ρ session for conversion requires the absolute mass
and confirmation of both multiplicities. Its old Fermi energy specifies the
new carrier excess only at that particular parameter vector.

The signed-mass and zero-temperature Fermi-energy formulas below describe
the legacy DPB parameterization.

## Units and signs

- Energies ($E_F$, $E_g$, μ) are in kelvin in the core, that is $E/k_B$. The app
  shows them in eV, at 1 K = 8.617333262 × 10⁻⁵ eV.
- Masses are in units of the electron mass mₑ. A positive mass is an electron
  (conduction) band, a negative mass a hole (valence) band.
- For a band with signed mass m and edge E₀, the reduced potential is
  $\eta = \operatorname{sign}(m)\,(\mu - E_0)/T$.
- In legacy DPB, band 1 is a valence band with its top at zero, so
  $\eta_1 = -\mu/T$. Band 2 starts at $E_g$, and the sign of m₂/m₁ sets its type:
  $\eta_2 = \operatorname{sign}(R)\,(\mu - E_g)/T$.
- $E_F$ sets the carrier density at T = 0. The chemical potential μ(T) is
  solved from it at each temperature.

## Fermi integrals

All transport properties are written with unnormalised Fermi integrals of the
reduced potential:

$$
F_j(\eta) = \int_0^\infty \frac{x^j}{1 + e^{x-\eta}}\,dx,
\qquad F_0(\eta) = \ln\!\left(1 + e^{\eta}\right).
$$

The core uses a stable logarithm for $F_0$ and fixed quadrature for the other
orders where needed. For $\eta \le -38$, the scaled integral
$e^{-\eta}F_j(\eta)$ is evaluated as $\Gamma(j+1)$ at floating-point precision.
Factoring out the common Boltzmann exponential keeps ratios of dilute-band
moments finite even when the raw moments round to zero. The
[P14 validation](https://github.com/pdasya/seeband-web/blob/main/packages/seeband_core/docs/golden-results.md#stable-fermi-integrals-p14)
reports the tested range.

## Scattering

For each pure mechanism the relaxation time is $\tau \propto T^{\beta} E^{\lambda - 1/2}$. The selected scattering law applies over the whole temperature range.

| Mechanism                 | λ   | β   | Explicit T factor of σ |
| ------------------------- | --- | --- | ---------------------- |
| Acoustic phonons (`acPh`) | 0   | −1  | $T^0\,F_0(\eta)$       |
| Alloy disorder (`dis`)    | 0   | 0   | $T^1\,F_0(\eta)$       |
| Ionized impurities        | 2   | 0   | $T^3\,F_2(\eta)$       |

σ also depends on T through η: a nondegenerate band at fixed density gives
$\sigma \propto T^{-3/2}$ for acoustic phonons, $T^{-1/2}$ for alloy disorder and
$T^{3/2}$ for ionized impurities.

The ionized option is the power law $\tau \propto E^{3/2}$, with no screening
or impurity-ionization model. Mixed acoustic phonons and alloy disorder
(`acPhDis`) is a separate selection that combines the two scattering rates
by Matthiessen's rule. Its dimensionless C and D are crossover temperatures
divided by 300 K. Mixed S-only does not use A, B, C or D. Saved mixed sessions keep
their scattering selection. In the mixed rows below, $f_i=T/(T+\Theta_i)$ with $\Theta_1=300C$ K and $\Theta_2=300D$ K.
The defaults differ between the models, as on the desktop. SPB starts at C = 100,
so $\Theta_1$ = 30 000 K and alloy disorder dominates over the whole range. DPB
starts at C = D = 1, so $\Theta_i$ = 300 K. The desktop showed the SPB value
divided by 100 but fitted it as it is; the web shows the value it fits.
See the [ionized-impurity formulas](https://github.com/pdasya/seeband-web/blob/main/packages/seeband_core/docs/ionized-impurities.md).

## Carrier density and chemical potential

The carrier density of one band and the density that $E_F$ sets at T = 0, in
m⁻³ as written; the core and the carrier-concentration curves report them in
cm⁻³:

$$
n(\mu, m, T) = 4\pi \left(\frac{2|m|\,m_e k_B T}{h^2}\right)^{3/2}
F_{1/2}\!\left(\frac{\operatorname{sign}(m)\,\mu}{T}\right),
$$

$$
n_0(E_F, m) = \frac{8\pi}{3}\left(\frac{2|m|\,m_e k_B |E_F|}{h^2}\right)^{3/2},
$$

with $n_0 = 0$ unless $\operatorname{sign}(m)\,E_F > 0$.

SPB solves $n(\mu, m, T) = n_0(E_F, m)$. Legacy DPB conserves the signed carrier
balance, with $r = R/N$, $s = \operatorname{sign}(r)$ and band 2's density mass
$rN^{5/3}$:

$$
-n(\mu, -1, T) + s\,n(\mu - E_g,\, rN^{5/3}, T)
= -n_0(E_F, -1) + s\,n_0(E_F - E_g,\, rN^{5/3}).
$$

Both are solved at each temperature with a bracketed method in μ/T. A point is
returned only if its normalised density residual is at most 10⁻¹²; otherwise the
whole calculation fails explicitly. In an intrinsic DPB gap, the solver normalises
the electron and hole densities before either can round to zero. Temperature order
and repeated points do not change the solution.

SPB needs $\operatorname{sign}(m)\,E_F > 0$. DPB with one valence and one
conduction band has a root even at zero net doping; two valence bands need
$E_F < \max(0, E_g)$. Zero mass and nonpositive degeneracies are invalid. These
checks do not establish fit quality.

For a positive gap between a valence and a conduction band, both carrier
populations vanish at 0 K throughout $0 \le E_F \le E_g$. With the other
parameters fixed, the same carrier target gives the same finite-temperature
$S$, $\rho$ and $R_H$ throughout the interval. SeeBand flags results in this
range because the transport data cannot locate $E_F$ within it.

## Legacy DPB mass and degeneracy

Let N = N₂/N₁. The public parameter m₂/m₁ is the signed mass ratio
R = m₂/|m₁|: its magnitude is the band-mass ratio and its sign the type of
band 2. The formulas receive r = R/N, the ratio weighted by inverse degeneracy,
converted once; starts, fitted values, bounds and uncertainties stay in R. The
carrier-density weight of band 2 is $N|R|^{3/2}$.

R is the ratio of single-valley band masses, not of density-of-states masses:
entering $m_{d,2}/m_{d,1}$ would count N twice. For R > 0 the transport weight
N/R holds for λ = 0 acoustic deformation-potential scattering with the same
deformation potential and elastic constant in both bands, isotropic valleys
whose band mass serves for both density and conduction, and no inter-valley or
inter-band scattering. Longitudinal transport uses the positive mass magnitude
|r|; carrier signs stay in occupations, signed density and Hall. See
[positive DPB weights](https://github.com/pdasya/seeband-web/blob/main/packages/seeband_core/docs/dpb-positive-weights.md).

Alloy disorder gives the same mass dependence for equal alloy potentials.

### Ionized impurities in explicit DPB

Define $M=|m_2|/|m_1|$ using single-valley masses and $N=N_2/N_1$. The
relative conductivity is $(NB/M) F_2(η_2)/F_2(η_1)$. B is a dimensionless
empirical normalization. In S-only, enter B (default 1) to hold it throughout
the fit. In either mode with resistivity, B can be held or fitted. The default
fit interval [0, 10000] includes $M^2$ across the default mass-ratio range
[0.01, 100]. Choose bounds appropriate for the sample.

For isotropic parabolic valleys with common impurity charge, density,
permittivity and a common constant screening factor, the Coulomb lifetime
scales as $\sqrt{m_i} E^{3/2}$. The conductivity prefactor then scales as
$N_i m_i$, giving $B=M^2$. Full Brooks–Herring screening depends on wavevector.
SeeBand uses the power-law approximation and keeps B independent of M.
A two-band fit to p-PbSe also used an adjustable relative mobility, which has
a different definition from B.
[Meyer and Bartoli](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.31.2353) ·
[Majdi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1002/adts.202000103) ·
[Chasapis et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.91.085207).

## Band diagram

The live diagram draws the local parabolic approximation for each band.
The wavevector $k$ is measured in $\mathrm{nm}^{-1}$ relative to the band
extremum. For a signed single-valley mass $m_b$,

$$
E(k)=E_0+\frac{\hbar^2 k^2}{2m_b}.
$$

The sign of $m_b$ sets the direction of the curvature. In the historical signed-mass DPB diagram, band 1
opens down from 0 and band 2 starts at $E_g$. For modes with Hall data, the
historical diagram interprets its first mass input as a density-of-states mass and draws the magnitude
of the single-valley mass $|m_{b,1}|=m_1/N_1^{2/3}$. The signed mass of band 2
is $m_{b,2}=R|m_{b,1}|$. In modes without Hall data, SPB or DPB, no data set
the size of a mass, so the diagram assumes $|m_{b,1}|=m_e$, keeping its sign, and
says that the curvature scale is illustrative. With Hall data the curvature
follows the current masses, which the data set only once a fit has run. The
Fermi level is a horizontal line at $E_F$. The API returns $E/k_B$ in K, and the
chart uses the selected energy unit.

$k$ is measured from each band's own extremum, so drawing both DPB bands at
$k = 0$ does not say that the gap is direct: band 2 can lie in another valley.

DPB Hall transport since [#252](https://github.com/pdasya/seeband-web/pull/252)
uses the single-valley mass and counts the absolute valley multiplicity separately.
For $N_1>1$ the historical diagram therefore draws a different curvature from
the mass used in that transport calculation. Explicit DPB uses the confirmed
single-valley mass directly for both. When opening a historical Hall session
with saved $N_1>1$, choose whether its saved mass is single-valley or all-valley
DOS. Core version `0.3.0` occurs with both conventions and does not establish
which one was used. For the older all-valley DOS convention, the migration
dialog divides the mass by the **saved** $N_1^{2/3}$ once and shows the
single-valley mass before confirmation. It uses the saved fit request's $N_1$
when present, otherwise the saved selection's $N_1$. Editing the multiplicity
for the new calculation does not change this interpretation of the saved mass.
For saved $N_1=1$, both conventions give the same mass and no choice is needed.
The selected convention and conversion inputs are preserved with the original
session provenance. This converts a saved parameter point; it does not guarantee
reproduction of Hall curves from versions with other historical formula changes.

## Seebeck coefficient

For one band, with λ of the chosen mechanism:

$$
S_i = -\operatorname{sign}(m_i)\,\frac{k_B}{e}
\left[\frac{(\lambda + 2)\,F_{\lambda+1}(\eta_i)}{(\lambda + 1)\,F_\lambda(\eta_i)} - \eta_i\right],
$$

that is $2F_1/F_0 - \eta$ for λ = 0 and $4F_3/(3F_2) - \eta$ for ionized
impurities. SPB's S depends only on η, so not on |m| or on the scattering scale.

For two bands the contributions are weighted by conductivity:

$$
S = \frac{F_\lambda(\eta_1)\,S_1 + w\,F_\lambda(\eta_2)\,S_2}{F_\lambda(\eta_1) + w\,F_\lambda(\eta_2)}
$$

| Mode and mechanism                  | Weight w            |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------- |
| S only, except explicit ionized DPB | $1/\lvert r \rvert$ |
| S only, explicit ionized DPB        | $NB/M$ (B held)     |
| S + ρ, acoustic phonons or ionized  | $B/\lvert r \rvert$ |
| S + ρ, alloy disorder               | $G/\lvert r \rvert$ |

For mixed scattering in modes with resistivity, $w=(B/\lvert r\rvert) f_2/f_1$.

The band contributions on the charts are the weighted terms, which add up to S,
not each band's own S.

## Conductivity and resistivity

ρ = 1/σ at the same parameters and temperatures. With $w$ as above and
$T_{300} = T/(300\ \mathrm{K})$:

| Mechanism          | SPB σ                     | DPB σ                                                           |
| ------------------ | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Acoustic phonons   | $A\,F_0(\eta)$            | $A\,[F_0(\eta_1) + (B/\lvert r\rvert)\,F_0(\eta_2)]$            |
| Alloy disorder     | $F\,T\,F_0(\eta)$         | $F\,T\,[F_0(\eta_1) + (G/\lvert r\rvert)\,F_0(\eta_2)]$         |
| Acoustic + alloy   | $A f_1 F_0(\eta)$         | $A [f_1 F_0(\eta_1) + (B/\lvert r\rvert) f_2 F_0(\eta_2)]$      |
| Ionized impurities | $A\,T_{300}^3\,F_2(\eta)$ | $A\,T_{300}^3\,[F_2(\eta_1) + (B/\lvert r\rvert)\,F_2(\eta_2)]$ |

A is a free conductivity amplitude in S/m, and F one in S/(m·K), a unit the app
does not yet label. B and G are dimensionless: band 2's amplitude relative to
band 1's. They are fitted amplitudes, not densities or scattering rates derived
from the sample. By default B is bounded to 0.9–1.1 and G to 0.5–2, both
editable, so band 2's weight comes mostly from r; a value at its bound is marked
so and is not determined by the data. The DPB band conductivities add up to σ.

## Hall coefficient

With $K = 3\pi^2\hbar^3 / \bigl(e\,(2k_B m_e)^{3/2}\bigr)$, one band gives

$$
R_{H} = -\operatorname{sign}(m)\,K\,\frac{T^{-3/2}}{|m|^{3/2}}\,
\frac{(2\lambda + \tfrac12)\,F_{2\lambda - 1/2}(\eta)}{(\lambda + 1)^2\,F_\lambda(\eta)^2},
$$

with η from μ(T). For λ = 0 this is

$$
R_H\,n\,e = -\operatorname{sign}(m)\,\frac{3\,F_{-1/2}(\eta)\,F_{1/2}(\eta)}{4\,F_0(\eta)^2},
$$

whose magnitude tends to 1 when degenerate and to 3π/8 when nondegenerate; for
ionized impurities it tends to 1 and to 315π/512 ≈ 1.93. In
SPB, the $R_H$ fit sets the absolute mass |m|. The Fermi integrals also support
reduced potentials above the former cutoff at η = 700.

For DPB, first evaluate the one-valley expression at the signed band
masses $-m_1$ and $m_2=R\,m_1$. Divide each Hall coefficient by its
own valley multiplicity $N_i$ to obtain $R_{H,i}$, then combine the bands as

$$
R_H = \frac{\sigma_1^2 R_{H,1} + \sigma_2^2 R_{H,2}}{(\sigma_1 + \sigma_2)^2},
$$

The DPB implementation includes $N_2/N_1$ in the relative band weights
and divides the combined Hall coefficient by $N_1$. The $R_H$ fit sets
the magnitude of the single-valley mass $m_1$ using the other parameters
from the S and ρ fits. In the Hall mode, each carrier density includes
its band's absolute multiplicity. Without $R_H$ data, carrier densities
are reference values computed with $m_1=1$ and $N_1=1$. See the
[DPB Hall prefactor](https://github.com/pdasya/seeband-web/blob/main/packages/seeband_core/docs/dpb-hall-prefactor.md)
and the [SPB Hall fit](https://github.com/pdasya/seeband-web/blob/main/packages/seeband_core/docs/spb-hall-fit.md).

## Thermal conductivity and Lorenz number

One band's Lorenz number:

$$
L_i = \left(\frac{k_B}{e}\right)^2
\left[\frac{(\lambda + 3)\,F_{\lambda+2}}{(\lambda + 1)\,F_\lambda}
- \left(\frac{(\lambda + 2)\,F_{\lambda+1}}{(\lambda + 1)\,F_\lambda}\right)^2\right],
$$

which is $(k_B/e)^2\,(3F_0F_2 - 4F_1^2)/F_0^2$ for λ = 0. It tends to
$(\lambda + 2)\,(k_B/e)^2$ when nondegenerate, so 2 for acoustic phonons and
alloy disorder and 4 for ionized impurities, and to
$L_0 = \tfrac{\pi^2}{3}(k_B/e)^2 = 2.443 \times 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{W\,\Omega/K^2}$
when degenerate.

The electronic thermal conductivity is $\kappa_e = L\,\sigma\,T$ for SPB, and for
DPB

$$
\kappa_e = T\,(L_1\sigma_1 + L_2\sigma_2)
+ T\,\frac{\sigma_1\sigma_2}{\sigma_1 + \sigma_2}\,(S_1 - S_2)^2,
$$

where the second term is the interband contribution and $S_i$ is each band's own S.
The core evaluates this term without forming a product of two small
conductivities. The chart shows absolute $L = \kappa_e/(\sigma T)$ on a $10^{-8}$ W·Ω/K² scale.
Text exports give each value directly in W·Ω/K². The two band curves show the
intrinsic $L_i$. With $w_i=\sigma_i/\sigma$, the total is
$w_1L_1+w_2L_2+w_1w_2(S_1-S_2)^2$. Electron and hole thermopowers have
opposite signs, so the last term can raise the total above both band curves and
above $L_0$. See [Thesberg et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.95.125206).
SeeBand offers this chart in modes with resistivity.

## Which data fit which parameters

| Fit mode      | SPB                                                  | Legacy DPB                                                        |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| S             | $E_F$                                                | m₂/m₁, $E_g$, $E_F$                                               |
| S + ρ         | $E_F$, then A (or F for alloy disorder), in one pass | m₂/m₁, $E_g$, $E_F$ and A, B (or F, G), repeated until they agree |
| S + ρ + $R_H$ | the same, then the band mass m                       | the same, then m₁                                                 |

For mixed scattering in either resistivity mode, the resistivity stage uses A and C for SPB and A, B, C and D for DPB.

In SPB S and S + ρ, the band mass is not fitted and only its sign matters.
Whether the data pin a parameter down is shown for each fit; a fit that
converges does not show that the model suits the sample.

For a new explicit DPB sample the S stage fits the positive mass ratio, signed
offset and Fermi energy within the selected bounds.
The ρ stage adds A and B (F and G for disorder). With Hall data, the user can
release the absolute mass. Each trial computes its carrier excess from the
trial energy and band parameters. Historical fixed-density sessions use the
saved excess throughout the fit. When the Hall mass or relative scattering weight couples the stages, a bounded search can seed
them with a vector that improves their residuals together. The solver
checks the separate stage objectives at the returned vector.

## Fitting methods

For SPB and historical DPB the default method, `verified-sequential-v2`, fits each stage by least squares
in the order above, solves the conductivity or Hall amplitude exactly for the
other parameters, works in public R throughout, and checks at the end that no
stage refit improves the result. `legacy-sequential-v1` reproduces the
desktop's fitting loop, with a threshold of 0.1 on the relative change of every
loop parameter and at most 1000 iterations in DPB S + ρ, or 20 with $R_H$; it
keeps the desktop's degeneracy limitation (`dpb_loop_degeneracy`). Ionized
impurities need the verified method. See
[verified sequential fitting](https://github.com/pdasya/seeband-web/blob/main/packages/seeband_core/docs/verified-sequential-fit.md).

Results name the core version, the fitting method and how the fit stopped. A
missing version is unknown and is not inferred.

## Initial values

A neural net gives legacy DPB starting values for acoustic phonons and alloy disorder,
from the first of its temperature ranges that the data cover. SPB starting
values come from the chosen mechanism. An estimate is a starting value only, not
evidence that a model fits the sample.

## Input data

Data come as separate files per quantity or as one table whose columns are
assigned. Columns are separated by spaces, tabs or commas; `#` starts a
comment. A decimal comma is refused.

| Quantity | SI unit | Recognised headers           | Other units              |
| -------- | ------- | ---------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| T        | K       | `T`, `T_K`, `temp`           | °C                       |
| S        | V/K     | `S`, `seebeck`               | µV/K, mV/K               |
| ρ        | Ω·m     | `rho`, `ρ`, `resistivity`    | Ω·cm, mΩ·cm, µΩ·m, µΩ·cm |
| σ        | S/m     | `sigma`, `σ`, `conductivity` | S/cm                     |
| $R_H$    | m³/C    | `RH`, `R_H`, `hall`          | cm³/C                    |

Headers are matched without case, except `T` and `S`. A column with no unit
given is read in SI. σ is used as ρ = 1/σ.

## Interpolation

Interpolation fits a degree-10 Chebyshev polynomial to all of a quantity's
points and evaluates it only where the requested fit range and that quantity's
measured range overlap, at about one point per 20 K. A 50–800 K request with
data over 25–750 K is interpolated over 50–750 K. An overlap of less than 20 K
(40 K where the data narrow the range) leaves that quantity out of the fit, and
the app says why. It never extrapolates. Interpolated points are not
independent measurements, and this does not cure overshoot or a poor model.

At low temperatures with both bands deeply dilute, the absolute conductivity
can still round to zero. The calculation then reports null resistivity and
the thermal conductivity can round to zero. Seebeck and Lorenz ratios can
remain finite because the common exponential cancels.

## Model limitations

These are assumptions of the approach, not errors:

- Bands are strictly parabolic, at most two, and $E_g$ and the masses do not
  depend on temperature.
- One scattering mechanism applies over the whole range. There is no residual
  resistivity: with acoustic phonons, ρ follows the carriers alone.
- From S alone SPB determines only $E_F$; the absolute mass comes from $R_H$. In
  DPB, N is set by the user, not fitted; from S it nearly trades off with R and
  $E_g$, so results hold for the N chosen.
- Lattice thermal conductivity is not modelled. SPB often holds only over a
  narrow temperature range.
